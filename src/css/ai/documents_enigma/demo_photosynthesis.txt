Photosynthesis is the process plants use to turn sunlight into chemical energy. It takes place mostly in the leaves, inside structures called chloroplasts, which contain a green pigment called chlorophyll. Chlorophyll absorbs sunlight, mainly in the red and blue parts of the spectrum, and reflects green light, which is why plants look green. During photosynthesis, the plant takes in carbon dioxide from the air through tiny pores called stomata and water from the soil through its roots. Using light energy, it converts these into glucose, a sugar the plant uses for food, and releases oxygen as a byproduct. This oxygen is what most animals, including humans, breathe. Photosynthesis is the foundation of nearly every food chain on Earth.
